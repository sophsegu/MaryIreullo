A LIFE OF FIRSTS

A Life Through the Years

From her childhood in Italy to the business she built and the community she served, Maria Ierullo's story is one of determination, compassion, and lasting impact. Explore the milestones, achievements, and recognitions that shaped her life and continue to preserve her legacy.

1920

Born in Catanzaro

Maria Ierullo was born in Catanzaro, Italy, in 1920. Her early life would eventually lead her across the Atlantic to Ottawa, where she would build a life devoted to family, business, and community.

Age 7

A New Life in Canada

Maria immigrated to Canada with her mother, Angela Maria Messina. Her father died shortly after the family's arrival, leaving Maria and her mother to build their lives in Ottawa.

Her Early Years

Learning to Build a Life

Maria left school before completing high school, worked full time, and continued studying at night. Her mother supported the family through embroidery, while Maria developed the independence and resourcefulness that would shape her future.

1946

The Loss of Her Mother

Angela Maria Messina died in 1946. The bond between Maria and her mother remained an important part of Maria's story and the legacy she would leave in Ottawa.

1950

Marriage and Family

Maria married Vincenzo “Jimmy” Ierullo in 1950. She would build her professional life alongside her responsibilities to her husband and family.

c. 1952

Exploring Real Estate

Before opening her own business, Maria briefly worked for another real-estate broker to learn about the profession and decide whether it was the right path for her.

c. 1952–1953

Pursuing Her Broker's Qualification

Maria began pursuing her broker's certification, taking steps toward a career in a field where Italian women were rarely represented. She would go on to pass the broker's examination with a score of 97 percent.

c. 1953

A Business Begins at Home

Maria started her own real-estate business in Centretown, initially working from the front room of her home. From these modest beginnings, she built a real estate brokerage serving families across Ottawa.

1950s

A Growing Real Estate Brokerage

Maria developed her business into a multilingual real-estate office serving Ottawa's diverse communities. By 1959, contemporary reporting described her Rochester Street office as employing ten full-time agents and noted its distinctive red, white, and green signs.

c. 1955–1956 onward

Supporting Mothers and Newcomers

Maria volunteered with prenatal services at the Civic Hospital, helping Italian-speaking and immigrant women navigate care. Her involvement reportedly continued for more than four years, providing interpretation and support to women during pregnancy.

1960s

A Voice in the Courtroom

Maria worked as a court interpreter, helping Italian-speaking residents understand and participate in legal proceedings. She also advocated for fair treatment and greater access to the legal system for those facing language barriers.

Mid-1960s

Standing With Her Neighbours

As urban renewal and expropriation affected Ottawa's Italian neighbourhoods, Maria became involved in advocating for residents facing the loss or displacement of their homes. She worked to help affected families seek fair treatment and compensation.

Throughout Her Career

Opening Doors for Others

Maria's real estate brokerage became more than a place to buy or sell a home. People turned to her for advice, help with paperwork, interpretation, and guidance through unfamiliar systems. Accounts of her life describe her willingness to help families, including by reducing or waiving fees when circumstances called for it.

December 25, 1988

Closing Her Real Estate Brokerage

Maria closed her real estate brokerage on Christmas Day, 1988, bringing to a close a major chapter in a career that had helped generations of Ottawa families establish themselves.

1990s–2000s

Her Story Begins to Be Preserved

Newspaper profiles and community publications documented Maria's real-estate career, service to immigrants, and contribution to Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community, helping preserve her experiences for future generations.

2002

The Italian Canadian Women's Filò

Maria was among the women honoured at Ottawa's first Italian Canadian Women's Filò, recognizing the contributions of women to the community and its cultural life.

2003

Presenza

Maria's life story became part of Presenza, an exhibition celebrating Italian-Canadian heritage at the Canadian Museum of Civilization. Contemporary reporting documented her inclusion while she was in failing health.

2004

The Maria Ierullo Award Is Established

The Maria Ierullo Award for Community Service was established in her honour, recognizing the values of service and community contribution associated with her life.

2007

A Leader of Her Generation

Progressive Choices included Maria in its “Decade of Leaders” feature, recognizing women whose lives and work helped shape their communities across the twentieth century.

2010

Her Story in Public Art

Maria's story became part of “Postcards from the Piazzas,” a public-art installation on Preston Street celebrating stories connected to Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community.

2025

Donne di Ottawa

Maria's legacy was included in “Donne di Ottawa,” an exhibition celebrating Italian-Canadian women in Ottawa. This recognition continued the work of preserving her story and those of other women who helped shape the community.

MARIA IERULLO · HER POETRY · HER VOICE

In Her Own Words

A voice preserved. A story remembered.

Maria's poetry gave expression to her memories, experiences, beliefs, and reflections on life. These original recordings offer the opportunity to hear Maria read her own work. In the recording of Nature's Symphony, she also shares her interpretation of the poem, allowing us to hear not only the words she wrote, but something of what they meant to her.

01

Nature's Symphony

Maria reads the opening stanza and continues by sharing her own meaning and interpretation of the poem.

Original family video recording · Shared with permission

Read the complete poem

The waves to me, sing a sweet serenade,
As it softly laps, near my quiet shade,
The wind follows up, with a sad refrain
To me it's like, the trees caught in the rain.

Then a whippoorwill takes up the cry
It’s like, the heartbreak in a mother’s sigh,
The blue sky above heads this display,
By reflecting its image on the bay.

The boat oars ripples, takes up the tune
While a lonely owl loudly calls the moon
Then all small insects of land and sea,
All strike up the sound in a different key.

The waves and wind to me it seems
Are softly calling the moon’s silvery beams
And all birds and insects of sea and lands
Then give thanks to God and join their hands

02

Our Canadian Pines

Maria reads the opening stanza of her poem. The complete poem is preserved below for visitors to read.

Original family video recording · Shared with permission

Read the complete poem

I am standing far below, and so alone,
While the trees above are hewn from solitude,
You lift your hands up high in solemn prayer,
Yet unheedful of my just, admiring stare.

Your splendor is the quiet, majestic kind,
Which aims to please, the soul, and human mind,
You neither waste a glance for me below,
For you seem to wish that I would kindly go.

Yet still alone I stand, a midget in bliss
For I wish to feel, the wind’s caressing kiss,
But still up high to God, your hands you raise
And I, in thanks to God, lift my voice in praise

These poems are among the works included in Who am I, I AM Maria. The recordings preserve Maria reading her own writing, offering a personal connection between the poems on the page and the voice that first brought them to life.
