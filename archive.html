2025
“Donne Di Ottawa” Exhibit Unveiled at the Centro Storico Italo-Canadese Italo-Canadese • By Cassandra Marsillo

On October 18, 2025, writer and project lead Anna Romano Milne hosted the unveiling of Donne Di Ottawa: Ci Siamo Anche Noi! (We Are Also Here!), a permanent exhibit at Ottawa’s Centro Storico Italo-Canadese. Supported by a City of Ottawa heritage grant, the project honours Italian-Canadian women who made lasting social, cultural, and political contributions to the nation's capital.

The inaugural exhibit features painted portraits by artist Lucia Alloggia and biographies for three local figures: Maria Ierullo (first Italian-descent real estate broker in Ottawa, advocate against expropriation in Little Italy), Rosa Tiezzi (advocate for interned men during WWII), and Jenny Prosperine (organized voting transport for local women in 1968).

“It is a reminder that women’s stories are worthy of recognition... It’s a message of inclusion that invites anyone connected to Italy through friendship, family and lived experience to be part of this evolving legacy.”

1950
“Other mother” Mary Ierullo The Ottawa Hospital Foundation • Moment 22/100

Mary Ierullo had her own children, but she was also the “other mother” to countless young women in Ottawa. After immigrating to Ottawa from Italy as a young girl in 1928, Mary was always inspired to help others.

In the 1950s, she saw young pregnant women from back home struggling, so despite not holding a formal role at the hospital, she helped set up the first prenatal clinic for immigrant women at the Civic. She held their hands, literally and figuratively, as they transitioned into motherhood in their new country.

“Deal from the heart … Open up to one another and understand each other. We are part of a community and knowing each other makes all the difference.”

2010
“Postcards from the Piazzas” City of Ottawa • Public Art Collection

Along Preston Street in Ottawa's Little Italy, the public art series Postcards from the Piazzas brings together stories, images, and symbols drawn from the history and culture of the local Italian-Canadian community. Created by artist cj fleury, the 15-piece installation was developed through extensive research and conversations with community members.

Among the stories represented in the Preston Street sculptures is the history of Maria Ierullo and her mother. The work connects their family history with the community's Italian-Canadian heritage, including Maria's work supporting immigrant women through a prenatal clinic, her career in real estate, and her family's sewing and business history.

“Each piece is a snapshot of someone’s story.”

The installation was unveiled in 2010 as part of the reconstruction of Preston Street. The sculptures were intended to preserve fragments of the neighbourhood's history and make those stories part of the public landscape.

2000
“An Angel from the ‘Italian Village’” Il Postino • By Zeljka Gaspar

Published in the October 2000 issue of Il Postino, this profile presents Mary Ierullo as a central figure in Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community. The article recounts her experiences as a court interpreter, community advocate, and real estate professional, including her opening of her own real estate office after becoming a licensed broker.

The profile also documents Mary's deep connection to the community she called the “Italian Village,” describing the ways she and her husband helped newcomers and supported families in Ottawa. Her work as an interpreter also brought her into contact with immigrants and allowed her to hear and understand the difficulties they faced.

“My role model all along the way was my mother, Angela Maria Messina.”

Mary describes her mother, Angela Maria Messina, as the person who taught her to stand on her own feet, help others, and have the courage to move forward. After coming to Ottawa, Angela opened an embroidery workshop on George Street, where Mary helped her for eight years until her mother's death in 1948.

Together, the article's account of Mary and her mother's lives provides an important record of the family history behind their contributions to Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community.

2003
“A Woman of Many Firsts” Centretown News • Katarzyna Pipin • March 28, 2003

In this March 28, 2003 profile, Katarzyna Pipin tells the story of Maria Ierullo through both her accomplishments and her personality. The article compares Maria's animated storytelling style to that of Sophia Petrillo from The Golden Girls, noting the shared Italian background and love of telling stories. Beneath the humour, however, is the story of a woman whose life was marked by service to her community.

Pipin highlights Maria's many firsts and her pioneering work in Ottawa, including her career in real estate, her involvement in establishing a prenatal clinic for immigrant women, and her efforts on behalf of homeowners facing expropriation. Her work extended beyond her professional career into the everyday needs of people in Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community.

The article also looks back to Maria's childhood. After her father died when she was young, her mother taught her to make the most of what she had and to use her abilities to build a home and a life for herself. Maria had once dreamed of becoming a missionary, but ultimately found another way to serve others through her work and community involvement.

Pipin recounts Maria's recognition by the Italian Canadian Women of the Village. The honour appears to have been deeply meaningful to Maria; the article describes her reaction to learning that she had been selected for the award. Her story was also being brought to a national audience through the Presenza exhibition at the Canadian Museum of Civilization, which highlighted the experiences and contributions of Italian Canadians.

A woman of many firsts whose professional life became inseparable from her commitment to helping others.

1997
“Deal from the Heart” Bob Vandenburg • Buzz • July 1997

Published in Buzz in July 1997, Bob Vandenburg's Deal from the Heart profiles Maria Ierullo and her pioneering career as a real estate broker and community advocate in Ottawa.

The article describes Maria as one of the first Italian women to become a real estate broker in Canada. After opening her Rochester Street office in 1959, she built a business that at one point employed sixteen agents, most of them women. Her clients included Italian immigrants as well as newcomers from Portugal, Britain, Russia, Ireland, and Mexico.

Beyond real estate, Maria was known for helping members of her community by providing free advice, translating documents, and interpreting for courthouses, schools, and churches. The article also describes her efforts to help newcomers and her broader commitment to lending a hand whenever she could.

Maria's distinctive real estate signs incorporated the red, white, and green of the Italian tricolour. According to the article, the design helped establish a visual tradition that other Italian-owned businesses in the neighbourhood subsequently followed.

“Deal from the heart. Open up to one another and understand each other. It makes all the difference.”

“The Toast of Little Italy: Centretown woman’s life story part of national exhibit” Crystal Rhyno • Centretown News • 2003

This 2003 article by Crystal Rhyno documents Maria Ierullo’s life and contributions to Ottawa’s Italian-Canadian community, including her role in helping immigrants transition to life in Canada through her work in real estate.

The article highlights Maria as one of the first Italian-Canadian women realtors in Ottawa. Her career helped open new doors for immigrants and provided support to newcomers as they established themselves in the city.

Maria’s story was also featured in the national Presenza exhibition, which displayed archival materials from her life and career. These included her real estate business calendar, listing agreements, house sales records, and hand-painted signage in Italian.

The article also records Maria’s recognition for her longstanding community involvement. She received a lifetime community service award from the Italian-Canadian Women of the Village for her contributions to the people of Preston Street.

2003
“From Italy to Canadians with Love” Tony Lofaro • Ottawa Citizen • June 13, 2003

Published in the Ottawa Citizen on June 13, 2003, Tony Lofaro’s article documents Maria Ierullo’s life and her contributions to Ottawa’s Italian-Canadian community.

The article highlights Maria’s role in the Presenza exhibition at the Museum of Civilization, which explored the Italian-Canadian immigrant experience and featured artifacts and archival materials from her life and career.

It describes Maria as the first female real estate broker in Ottawa and possibly in Canada. Beyond real estate, she helped establish the first prenatal clinic for immigrant women at the Civic Hospital and worked as a freelance court interpreter, helping Italian immigrants navigate the court system.

Maria also worked with priests at St. Anthony’s Church to welcome new immigrants to Ottawa. During the mid-1960s, she advocated for homeowners whose properties were being expropriated, fighting for them to receive fair market value for their homes.

The article portrays Maria as deeply religious and committed to helping others. Although she had once considered entering a convent, she instead found her calling through community service and helping people in need. It also recognizes her as a champion of women’s rights.

2007
“Decade of Leaders” Progressive Choices • Spring 2007

The Spring 2007 edition of Progressive Choices celebrates 100 women whose lives and accomplishments helped shape the previous century. Maria Ierullo was selected to represent the 1950s.

The feature recounts Maria’s immigration from Italy to Ottawa and the challenges she faced while raising three children and caring for her husband during his health difficulties. It describes how she went on to become the first Italian woman to pass her real estate examination in the 1950s.

Maria became a trusted friend and adviser to many members of Ottawa’s Italian-Canadian community, helping immigrants find homes and establish themselves in Canada. She took particular pride in the fact that none of her clients lost their homes for financial reasons.

The article also highlights Maria’s role as a founder of the Independent Realtors Association and her many years serving as a court interpreter for Italians facing legal difficulties.

Through her real estate work, community involvement, and service as an interpreter, Maria became a source of guidance and support for many newcomers to Ottawa.

The directory page describes Maria as the first Italian woman real estate broker and the first woman court interpreter. The account also highlights Maria's community advocacy, including her work establishing a women's prenatal clinic in the 1950s, her efforts on behalf of homeowners affected by expropriation, and her involvement in establishing zoning laws in the region.

Her community service was formally recognized by the Italian Canadian Women of the Village, who honoured her with a lifetime service award. The page also notes that Maria's story was featured by the Museum of Civilization in an exhibition about Italian Canadians and records that she received a papal blessing.

2014
“Maria Ierullo Award for Community Service” Italian Telephone Directory of Ottawa • 2014

This page from the 2014 Italian Telephone Directory of Ottawa provides a brief account of Maria Ierullo's life in connection with the Maria Ierullo Award for Community Service, sponsored by Maria and her daughter Angela and presented through the Ottawa Italian Canadian community.

According to the directory, Maria was born in Catanzaro, Italy, in 1920 and immigrated to Canada with her mother at the age of seven. The death of her father shortly after the family's arrival in Canada profoundly influenced her desire to help others. Although she left school before completing high school, Maria continued her education by studying at night while working full time.

She went on to earn her real estate broker's licence and opened her own real estate firm in the mid-1950s. She remained in business until 1988. The directory emphasizes her commitment to making home ownership accessible to her clients and states that none of her clients lost their property for financial reasons.

The account also describes Maria's community service as being rooted in her Catholic faith and her active involvement with St. Anthony's Church. Among her community activities were organizing a prenatal clinic for young Italian women and helping homeowners whose properties were being expropriated in the Booth Street and Preston Street area during the 1960s.

In recognition of her lifelong commitment to the community, Maria and her daughter Angela donated the Maria Ierullo Award for Community Service, creating a lasting connection between Maria's legacy and the recognition of community service within Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community.

Undated
“Maria Ierullo: Translation & Community Services” Brian J. Porte • Newspaper Article

This undated newspaper article by Brian J. Porte examines Maria Ierullo's work providing translation, settlement assistance, and community services to immigrants in Ottawa's Dalhousie Ward in the years following the Second World War.

The article describes the challenges faced by many immigrants arriving in Ottawa with little or no knowledge of Canada's official languages. With community resources unable to meet the demand for assistance, many newcomers were largely left to navigate unfamiliar systems on their own. The Church of St. Anthony of Padua is also identified as an important part of the settlement network serving the community.

Within this environment, Maria Ierullo is presented as someone who understood how to navigate Ottawa's institutions and could connect newcomers with both the Italian-Canadian and wider community. Porte describes her as bringing together the drive, knowledge, skills, contacts, commitment, and personality that enabled her to build a pioneering career as a real estate broker, translator, and community activist.

The article emphasizes that much of Maria's assistance to newcomers was provided on a volunteer basis, highlighting a dimension of her community service that extended beyond her professional work.

A pioneering career in real estate, translation, and community service.

c. 2003
“Maria Ierullo and the Presenza Story” Jane Mosgrove • Newspaper Article

Mounted on fibreboard, this newspaper article by Jane Mosgrove reflects on Maria Ierullo's life and her inclusion in Presenza, the national exhibition celebrating the experiences and contributions of Italian Canadians. Written when Maria was in failing health, the article portrays her as a deeply humble woman who remained passionate about her community and its history.

Mosgrove recalls Maria speaking passionately about the urban renewal evictions that affected the Preston Street area during the mid-1960s. Despite her declining health and reliance on a walker, Maria is remembered as energetic and determined. Her story was also recognized by the Italian Women of the Village and highlighted in the Museum of Civilization's Presenza exhibition.

The article traces Maria's early life and her relationship with her mother, Angela, whom she helped with her embroidery business. After her mother's death in 1946, Maria entered a convent in Ottawa, believing that religious life was her calling. A sister at the convent encouraged her to consider that God might have other plans for her. Maria subsequently married Vincenzo “Jimmy” Ierullo in 1950.

Her later career became closely connected with helping immigrants establish themselves in Ottawa. As a real estate broker, Maria employed 16 agents, 14 of whom were women from different backgrounds and cultures. The article recalls that she frequently waived her fees to help immigrants secure housing and held Sunday evening gatherings where new home buyers could receive assistance completing mortgage-related paperwork.

Maria's community work extended beyond real estate. She worked as a court translator and, during the 1950s, translated for workers within unions, helping ensure that immigrant workers could understand proceedings and were fairly represented. The article also recalls that people continued to stop Maria in the community to ask for help with their real estate needs, even later in her life.

“I think I must give something back for all I've been given. Besides, I could not have done what I did without God's help and everyone else too.”

2002
“Ottawa Italian Canadian Women's Filò” Nicole Valentinuzzi • April 2002

In April 2002, Nicole Valentinuzzi reported on the first Ottawa Italian Canadian Women's Filò, an event celebrating women whose contributions had shaped Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community. Maria Ierullo and Jennie Prosperine were honoured for their work within the Ottawa community, while the Honourable Maria Minna was also recognized as the first Italian-Canadian woman to serve as a federal cabinet minister.

The event was organized by Ariella DalFarra Hostetter and Anna Chiappa. The article describes Maria Ierullo as having a longstanding reputation for excellence within Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community and highlights her pioneering career in real estate, including becoming the first woman to earn a real estate broker's licence in the mid-1950s.

The article places the women's achievements within a broader reflection on the strength and continuity of Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community. Its closing remarks emphasize faith, family, and the values passed down through generations, reflecting the religious and cultural traditions of the community represented at the Filò.

Maria Ierullo was honoured for her longstanding contribution to the Ottawa Italian-Canadian community.

2003
“A New Look at Italian Canadian Heritage” Il Postino • February 2003

Published in Il Postino in February 2003, this article previews the opening of Presenza, a new exhibition at the Canadian Museum of Civilization dedicated to Italian-Canadian heritage. The article reports on preparations within Ottawa's Italian-Canadian community as the museum prepared to unveil the exhibition.

Presenza was presented as an opportunity to explore the experiences, contributions, traditions, and heritage of Italian Canadians, particularly the generation that helped establish communities across Canada after the Second World War.

The article provides important contemporary context for the exhibition's arrival in Ottawa and documents the anticipation within the local Italian-Canadian community before the exhibit opened. Maria Ierullo's story would subsequently be highlighted as part of Presenza, connecting her personal history and community work to the broader story of Italian-Canadian life in Canada.
